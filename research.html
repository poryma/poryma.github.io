Research

My research examines social determinants of health and mortality, with a focus on suicide, mental health, community violence, time use, and environmental justice. I use quantitative and computational methods to study how social environments shape individual and population health outcomes, primarily in South Korea and Latin America. Increasingly, I bring a social welfare and social policy lens to this work, asking how services and prevention programs can reach the people most at risk.

Published in Peer-Reviewed Journals

  • Does Exposure to Community Violence Predict Higher Suicide Rates? The Case of Mexico
    Caudillo, Mónica L. and Jaein Lee.
    Population and Development Review, 2026. Featured in PNAS Journal Club, September 4, 2026.
    ↗ https://doi.org/10.1111/padr.70080
    Key finding: Local homicide spikes were followed by a short-lived rise in suicide, strongest in the same month and driven mainly by men (municipal death records, 2000–2019).
  • Racial-Ethnic Disparities in Dyadic Pregnancy Intentions Preceding Births in the United States
    Caudillo, Mónica L., Seungwan Kim**, Jaein Lee, and Jingwen Liu**.
    Population Research and Policy Review, 43(2), 2024.
    ↗ https://doi.org/10.1007/s11113-024-09860-z
    Key finding: About a quarter of U.S. births (26%) were conceived when partners disagreed about the pregnancy, with large racial-ethnic differences.
  • Changing Attitudes Toward Homosexuality in South Korea, 1996–2018
    Lin, Zhiyong and Jaein Lee.*
    Social Science Research, 118, 2024.
    ↗ https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssresearch.2023.102972
    Key finding: The share of Koreans saying homosexuality is never justifiable fell from 67% in the 1990s to 24% in the latest wave; among young adults, women were more accepting than men.
  • Why is the Korean Girls' Suicidal Ideation Rate Higher than the Boys' Rate? The Role of the Gender Heterogeneity of Peer Groups
    Bae, Youngjoon and Jaein Lee.
    PLoS ONE, 18(9), 2023.
    ↗ https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0290072
    Key finding: Girls were about twice as likely as boys to report suicidal impulses (37.7% vs. 18.5%), and having different-gender friends was linked to higher risk, mainly for girls.
  • Compliance, Procrastination and Refusal: American COVID-19 Vaccination Trust and Value Orientation
    Tzeng, Rueyling, Fang-Yi Huang, and Jaein Lee.*
    Vaccine, 41(34), 4950–4957, 2023.
    ↗ https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vaccine.2023.06.060
    Key finding: Low trust in vaccine effectiveness and seeing vaccination as a personal choice were the strongest predictors of delaying or refusing COVID-19 vaccination, more than politics or health risk.
  • Community Violence and the Stability of Marriages and Cohabitations in Mexico
    Caudillo, Mónica L. and Jaein Lee.
    Social Forces, 102(1), 287–309, 2023.
    ↗ https://doi.org/10.1093/sf/soac140
    Key finding: For less educated women, a recent rise in local violence was associated with a 71% higher risk that their first union dissolved; no clear link for more educated women.
  • Local Violence and Transitions to Marriage and Cohabitation in Mexico
    Caudillo, Mónica L. and Jaein Lee.
    Journal of Marriage and Family, 85(2), 345–369, 2023.
    ↗ http://doi.org/10.1111/jomf.12890
    Key finding: For less educated women, a recent rise in local violence was associated with about 28% lower chances of entering marriage as a first union.
  • Work Environment and Workers' Smoking: The Impact of Affect and Social Support Among Korean Workers
    Gu, Gihwan, Jaein Lee, and Myoung-Jin Lee.
    Journal of Asian Sociology, 50(2), 273–298, 2021.
    ↗ http://doi.org/10.21588/dns.2021.50.2.001
    Key finding: Less job autonomy and less flexible working hours were associated with smoking, partly through negative emotions; social support was associated with less smoking.
  • An Exploratory Approach to Changes in Jobs for Female Workers: A Focus on Data on Job Change from the Korean Longitudinal Survey of Women and Families
    Lee, Myoung-Jin, So-Young Ahn and Jaein Lee.
